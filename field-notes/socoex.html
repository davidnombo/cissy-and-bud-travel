Southern Colorado Expedition

A “full-scale model” for PourHouse, long travel, and off-grid camping.

Field NoteSouthern Colorado Expedition

We discovered that U.S. 50 runs from Cincinnati to Pueblo. So we traveled roughly 1,300 miles “without turning,” then set out to drive every labeled scenic highway we could find in southern Colorado.

Draft status: This is an intentional preview built from David’s route skeleton. Exact dates, mileage, the official Mount Blanca BLM location, and Cassie’s perspective remain open in the canonical document.

Testing everything

SoCoEx was not only about testing PourHouse’s mechanical and electrical systems. We were testing how to pack it, how to sleep for multiple consecutive nights, how to live off grid, and how the new 360 camera could document the roads around us.

Our first off-grid night was on BLM land near Mount Blanca. Free-range cattle surrounded PourHouse, creating a deep inventory of photographs and a scene reserved for Cassie to tell in the final interview.

All the scenic roads

We drove the Million Dollar Highway and every labeled scenic highway we could fit into southern Colorado. Spencer and Ria met us for a long weekend in Ouray, where we shifted into a hotel and explored together. We also saw a bear in Ouray—another scene waiting for both perspectives before it becomes finished prose.

The Uncompahgre National Forest delivered a small but symbolic milestone. A route finally required the first deliberate push of PourHouse’s 4x4 button. Earlier trips had repeatedly ended at signs the minivan could not pass. This time, the capable Alaska rig we had imagined was underneath us.

The purpose of a full-scale model

The expedition was designed to reveal problems while there was still time to solve them. Off-grid nights, consecutive sleep, high roads, storage, cameras, meals, and daily movement all had to work together. Success did not mean learning that everything was perfect. It meant learning how Cassie and David—and PourHouse—behaved over a complete journey.

The final Field Note will add Cassie’s interview, selected photographs, exact road names, and the lessons that carried directly into preparation for The Transcontinental Odyssey.
