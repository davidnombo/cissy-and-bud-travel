SoCoEx · PourHouse · August 2025

Southern Colorado
Expedition

Approximately 4,000 miles and a full-scale model for long travel and off-grid camping in PourHouse.

Cassie + DavidInterview outline
The outlineUS-50Off gridScenic ColoradoOuray

We discovered that US-50 runs from Cincinnati to Pueblo. So we traveled all 1,300 miles “without turning.”

SoCoEx was not merely a systems check. We called it a full-scale model for long travel and off-grid camping. We tested everything: how to pack, how to sleep multiple consecutive nights, how to live away from hookups and how to document the experience with a new 360 camera.

All the way on US-50

The opening premise was simple enough to become a joke and strange enough to become an expedition. US-50 could carry us from Cincinnati to Pueblo, about 1,300 miles, without turning off the highway.

Mount Blanco BLM

Our first off-grid night was at Mount Blanco BLM [official place name to verify]. Free-range cattle surrounded PourHouse, giving us an immediate photo story and a memorable first test of sleeping alone on public land.

The scene details are intentionally reserved for Cassie’s interview. What is confirmed: the van worked, the cattle arrived and plenty of photographs exist.

Every scenic road we could find

We drove the Million Dollar Highway and every labeled scenic highway we could identify in southern Colorado. The goal was not efficiency. It was immersion—and a serious rehearsal for the much larger journeys PourHouse was built to handle.

The first 4x4 button push

In Uncompahgre National Forest, the route required our first real use of four-wheel drive. It was a small physical action with symbolic weight: PourHouse carried us beyond one of the limits that had repeatedly stopped the minivan.

For the first time, the 4x4 sign was an invitation.

Ouray with Spencer and Ria

Spencer and Ria joined us in Ouray for a long weekend. We stayed in a hotel while they were with us, trading off-grid testing for family time and access to one of Colorado’s most dramatic mountain towns.

Ouray also produced a bear sighting—an encounter still waiting for its full scene and Cassie’s perspective.
