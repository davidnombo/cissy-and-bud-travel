About us

Cassie & David.
Off script.

We were regular people with demanding careers and a plan to work well into our sixties. Then a summer off became something entirely different.

We’re Cassie and David. We were business professionals in marketing research, calling on some of the largest retailers and manufacturers in the country, building teams and meeting our goals. We were good at the work. We also understood there was no end game—only more work, more promotions and more headaches.

We didn’t design an elegant retirement. We quit work and agreed to take one summer off to recharge. Instead of relaxing, we landed a cancer diagnosis. Cassie’s treatment changed the meaning of time, but it did not hand us a tidy plan. We simply kept moving without one.

We escaped the infinite work doom cycle.

For years, this life remained an experiment. One possible outcome was that we would have to return to conventional work. The Prius took us through Utah. A homemade minivan camper carried us across the country. PourHouse now lets us stay off-grid for days at a time. Only recently have we begun to believe that we may never go back.

Freedom, to us, is simple: no schedule, few bills and almost no mail. It is not endless leisure. It is the ability to define our days for ourselves.

We dream together

We often lock onto a trip years before we leave, dropping points of interest into Google Maps until a possibility becomes a route. Once we are moving, reservations are usually the only schedule. In PourHouse, even those have become rare.

David plans much of the route, finds wild camping and manages the van. Cassie navigates from the passenger seat, manages our social calendar and keeps us connected to our community through social media. We share the dream, the decisions and the miles.

PourHouse makes it possible to immerse ourselves in travel: places to see, meals, exercise, time together and time with the people we meet. Our bed, bathroom, refrigerator, food and power travel with us. That keeps the journey both flexible and financially efficient.

Why share it?

People in our lives have asked us to share more. With The Transcontinental Odyssey upon us, why wait to build?

We are not in a position to evaluate anyone else’s life or recommend improvements. We would rather challenge each person to examine their own: Am I happy? What else could I do? Am I following expectations or my own choices? What are my limits? How do I get after my bucket list?

By definition, we no longer vacation. Vacation is a break from daily routine. We have shredded routine. Adventure is our daily life.

— Cassie and David
