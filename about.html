Cassie and David

We escaped the infinite work doom cycle.

Not with a grand retirement plan. We took a summer off to recharge, landed a cancer diagnosis instead, and kept moving.

The short version

We are real people building freedom as we see it.

We were marketing-research professionals at Information Resources, now Circana. We called on major retailers and manufacturers, built teams, met our goals, and understood that the reward for success was more work, more promotions, and more headaches.

We did not plan a retirement. We planned one summer away. Cassie’s cancer diagnosis changed the shape of that pause, and the old script never reclaimed us. For years, our unconventional life remained an experiment with a possible return to work. Only recently did we understand that we were not going back.

We no longer vacation. A vacation is a break from daily routine. We have shredded routine.

We dream together, sometimes years ahead, filling Google Maps with possibilities. David plans much of the route, finds wild camping, and manages PourHouse. Cassie navigates, manages our social calendar, and keeps us connected to our community. Reservations are often the only schedule—and in PourHouse, even those are becoming rare.

PourHouse was also the name of our boat. Today it is our Revel and our mobile base: bed, bathroom, refrigerator, food, power, and the freedom to immerse ourselves in places rather than commute in and out of them. Along the way, we make a point of visiting the bars, taverns, coffee shops and other businesses that share the PourHouse name.

We share this life because people we know kept asking for more. We are not here to judge anyone else’s choices. We only hope the stories prompt useful questions: Am I happy? Am I doing what is expected, or what I want? Which limits are real? How do I get after my bucket list?

Cassie’s About Us interview is still to come. This version uses David’s completed interview and will be rebalanced when Cassie adds her voice.
