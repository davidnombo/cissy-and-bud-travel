Life on the road

A few honest answers.

The practical questions behind the photographs.

Have a question we didn’t answer? Send us a note.

Send Us a Note ↗
1. Do you really live in that van?

Yep. It’s our bedroom, kitchen, living room, garage, office, and occasionally the source of confusion about where we put something.

2. How long are you traveling?

We’ve already traveled almost a year, on and off, in the Field Notes. But this trip? About a year continuously. After that, we’ll probably come up with another bad idea.

3. Where are you going right now?

Warsaw, Kentucky to Jenner, California. Jenner to Key West, Florida. Key West to Deadhorse, Alaska. And Deadhorse back to Jenner.

4. Do you have an actual route planned?

Absolutely. Several of them. They tend to become obsolete shortly after we leave.

5. How many miles will you drive?

We expect the Transcontinental Odyssey to last 52 weeks and about 50,000 miles.

6. Why travel in a van instead of an RV?

We like being small enough to get almost anywhere, but large enough to carry a refrigerator and make questionable decisions comfortably. Oh, and the 4x4 helps a lot.

7. What kind of van is it?

A Winnebago Revel built on a Mercedes-Benz Sprinter 4x4. Basically a tiny apartment wearing hiking boots.

8. Where do you sleep?

In the van. Preferably somewhere beautiful, quiet, legal, and free—in roughly that order.

9. Do you stay in campgrounds every night?

Nope. We love dispersed camping on public land. Sometimes we use campgrounds. Occasionally we even pay for one.

10. How do you find places to camp?

Maps, apps, public-land resources, recommendations, satellite imagery, road signs, and occasionally blind optimism.

11. Do you have a bathroom in the van?

Yes. This question usually comes surprisingly early in conversations.

12. Do you have a shower?

Yep. We also have rivers, lakes, campground showers and an evolving definition of “clean.”

13. How do you get water?

Campgrounds, parks, RV fills, friends, businesses and other legitimate sources. Water becomes much more interesting when you have to carry all of it.

14. How do you get electricity?

Batteries, solar, and occasionally shore power. The house systems are remarkably efficient, and generally the solar gets the work done.

15. How do you get internet on the road?

Cellular service when we can get it and Starlink when we can’t. There are very few places left where David can successfully claim he “couldn’t get the email.”

16. How do you do laundry?

Usually campgrounds, occasionally laundromats, and whenever possible, a friend’s house. Good friends have washers and dryers.

17. What do you eat?

Surprisingly normal food. We cook in the van, eat locally when something looks interesting, and have learned that almost anything tastes better after being outside all day.

18. How do you decide where to go next?

Weather, scenery, wildlife, national parks, recommendations and whatever catches our attention. The itinerary gets a vote, but not a veto.

19. What’s your favorite place you’ve visited?

We have a few favorites, but we’re hoping the list keeps changing. In fact, if we come home from Alaska with the same favorites we left with, we probably did something wrong.

20. What’s the best part of traveling this way?

Waking up every day with your best friend, knowing you don’t have to leave—and when you do, it’s because another adventure is waiting.

21. What’s the hardest part?

Once we’re rolling and find our groove, life on the road is surprisingly easy. The hardest part may be deciding which adventure comes next. Of course, Mother Nature gets a vote—and occasionally, so does the van.

22. Do you ever get tired of each other?

Of course not. Next question.

23. Is van life cheaper than living in a house?

It can be. Then you add diesel, restaurants, national parks, repairs, gear and the occasional “we may never be here again” purchase.

24. Why is it called PourHouse Life?

Because the name followed us from our old life into this one. The house got smaller. The life got bigger.
